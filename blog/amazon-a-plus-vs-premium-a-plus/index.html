Amazon A+ Content vs Premium A+: Is the Upgrade Worth It?

By Prathna Jeswani

Most brands treat Premium A+ as an obvious upgrade. Bigger images, video, interactive modules — of course it converts better. Then they build it, ship it, and watch the conversion rate move by almost nothing.

The upgrade is real. What is not real is the assumption that it lifts every listing equally. Premium A+ amplifies whatever your creative is already doing. If the underlying creative answers buyer objections, more space to do that is worth a great deal. If it does not, Premium A+ gives you a larger canvas for the same unanswered questions.

What Standard A+ Already Gives You

Standard A+ Content replaces the plain-text product description with a set of image-and-text modules. You get comparison charts, feature callouts, lifestyle imagery, and brand context, laid out at roughly 970 pixels wide.

Amazon’s own guidance puts the conversion lift from adding A+ Content at 3 to 10% on average. That range is wide for a reason: the lift depends far more on what the modules say than on the fact that they exist. A listing that goes from no A+ to a well-built comparison chart and a size-and-scale module can move well past 10%. A listing that goes from no A+ to three brand-story panels about company heritage often moves close to zero.

Standard A+ is free for brand-registered sellers. There is no scenario in 2026 where a registered brand should be running listings without it.

What Premium A+ Actually Adds

Premium A+ is the same idea with a bigger toolbox:

  • Wider modules — roughly 1464 pixels instead of 970, so images render sharper and detail survives on desktop
  • Video modules — embedded video inside the description area, not just the image carousel
  • Interactive comparison tables — sortable and expandable rather than a flat image
  • Hover hotspots — callouts layered onto a product image, revealed on interaction
  • Image carousels — several images in the space one used to occupy
  • Q&A and FAQ accordions — objection handling that does not consume vertical space until the shopper asks

Amazon’s guidance puts Premium A+ conversion lift at up to roughly 20%, against the 3 to 10% for standard. Treat that as a ceiling under good execution, not a number you inherit by switching the format on.

What It Costs

Premium A+ used to carry a substantial annual fee. Amazon has since made it available at no additional cost to sellers who meet qualifying criteria — typically an approved Brand Registry, standard A+ published across your catalogue, and a live Brand Store. Eligibility rules change, so confirm your current status in Seller Central rather than relying on any article, including this one.

The real cost has shifted from licence fee to production. Premium modules need assets that standard modules do not: video, higher-resolution imagery that holds up at 1464 pixels, and hotspot artwork built to be read in layers. Reusing your standard A+ images at the larger size is the single most common way brands make Premium A+ look worse than what they replaced.

The Conversion Math

Whether the upgrade pays back is arithmetic, not opinion. Take a listing doing 1,000 sessions a month at a 12% conversion rate and a $40 selling price. That is 120 orders, $4,800 in revenue.

A 2 percentage point lift takes conversion to 14%. That is 140 orders and $5,600 — an extra $800 a month from the same traffic. Against a one-time production cost, the payback window is short.

Now run the same lift on a listing doing 80 sessions a month. Two points of conversion is roughly one and a half extra orders. The production cost never comes back.

This is why Premium A+ is a portfolio decision, not a brand-wide one. The upgrade belongs on the listings that already have traffic.

Who Should Upgrade, and Who Should Not

Upgrade if

  • The listing already receives meaningful traffic and the conversion rate is the constraint, not the impressions
  • The product is considered rather than impulse — higher price, technical specs, or a real comparison set
  • Buyers ask the same questions repeatedly in reviews and Q&A, and you have space to answer them
  • You genuinely have the assets, or budget for them

Do not upgrade yet if

  • Standard A+ is missing or half-built on the same ASIN — fix that first, it is free and the lift is larger
  • The hero image and image stack have not been optimised — those decide the click, and no amount of description-area work recovers a click you never got
  • Traffic is low enough that a conversion lift cannot pay for the production
  • Your only plan is to re-export existing images at a larger size

What Most Brands Get Wrong

Three failures account for most disappointing Premium A+ rollouts.

Upscaling instead of reshooting. A 970-pixel-wide asset stretched to 1464 pixels looks soft. Shoppers do not articulate this, they simply read the listing as lower quality than the one next to it. If the assets cannot be produced at the larger size, standard A+ with sharp images beats Premium A+ with soft ones.

Using video as decoration. A video module that shows the product rotating on a white background occupies premium real estate and answers nothing. A video showing the product being installed, worn, or used at actual scale removes a real purchase objection.

Treating modules as brand story. The description area is where a shopper who is already interested goes to resolve doubt. Company history, founder narrative, and mission statements rarely resolve doubt. Sizing, materials, compatibility, comparison to the obvious alternative, and what is in the box do.

The Order of Operations

If you are deciding where creative budget goes this quarter, the sequence that produces the most return is consistent:

  1. Fix the hero image. It decides the click, and everything downstream depends on getting one.
  2. Build a complete image stack that answers the top objections in order.
  3. Publish standard A+ on every ASIN. It is free.
  4. Upgrade to Premium A+ on the listings with traffic, with assets built for the format.

Brands routinely attempt step four while step one is still broken. That is the most reliable way to spend money on Amazon creative and see nothing happen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Premium A+ Content free?

For sellers who meet Amazon’s qualifying criteria, there is no additional licence fee. Qualification typically requires Brand Registry, standard A+ published across your catalogue, and a live Brand Store. Eligibility rules change, so confirm your current status in Seller Central.

How much does A+ Content increase conversion?

Amazon’s guidance puts standard A+ at 3 to 10% on average and Premium A+ at up to roughly 20%. Actual results depend heavily on what the modules communicate. Well-built comparison and sizing modules outperform brand-story modules by a wide margin.

Should every product have Premium A+?

No. The upgrade pays back on listings that already have traffic and where conversion is the constraint. On low-traffic listings, the production cost outlives the return. Publish standard A+ everywhere; upgrade selectively.

Can I reuse my standard A+ images for Premium A+?

Only if they were produced at high enough resolution to hold up at roughly 1464 pixels wide. Upscaled assets look soft next to competitors with native-resolution imagery, and that perceived quality gap costs more than the format gains.

What matters more, A+ Content or listing images?

Listing images. The hero image and image stack decide whether a shopper clicks and whether they stay. A+ Content works on shoppers who already arrived. Fix the images first.
