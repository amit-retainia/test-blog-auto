Amazon Product Title Guidelines: What’s Required, What Gets Suppressed

By Prathna Jeswani

Amazon doesn’t email you when a title breaks the rules. It just suppresses the listing, quietly, and you find out when traffic drops. The title guidelines aren’t suggestions — they’re the checklist Amazon’s automated systems run against every time a listing updates. Here’s what actually gets enforced.

What every title needs, regardless of category

A handful of elements are expected almost everywhere, even though the exact character limit and formatting rules shift by category:

  • Brand name, matching exactly what’s registered on the listing
  • What the product actually is, in plain language a shopper would search for
  • The key distinguishing attribute: size, color, quantity, or model number

Skip any of these and the title reads as incomplete, even before Amazon’s automated checks flag it.

Character limits vary by category, and they’re enforced

Most categories cap titles at 200 characters, but several — Grocery and some Health & Personal Care subcategories among them — allow far less. Going over the limit doesn’t get a warning; it gets the listing suppressed until the title is shortened. Check the specific style guide for your category in Seller Central before publishing, not after.

The content that triggers a suppression

These are the patterns Amazon’s automated review catches most often:

  • ALL CAPS words used for emphasis rather than as part of an actual brand or model name
  • Promotional language: “best seller,” “free shipping,” “% off,” “sale”
  • Symbols that aren’t part of the product name itself: !, $, ~, *, £
  • Subjective claims Amazon can’t verify: “highest quality,” “guaranteed”
  • The seller name repeated redundantly alongside the brand name

None of these require a manual review to catch. They’re rule-based, which means a title that passed review last month can still get flagged today if Amazon updates the pattern list.

Formatting rules that are easy to miss

Beyond banned words, Amazon enforces formatting details that sellers overlook:

  • No HTML tags or special characters pasted in from a spreadsheet
  • Numerals should be written as digits, not spelled out (“6-Pack,” not “Six-Pack”)
  • Measurements need a unit of measure attached — a bare number isn’t enough
  • No decorative punctuation used to separate title sections, like pipes or multiple dashes in a row

Category-specific rules override the general guidelines

Apparel, Grocery, and Electronics each have their own required title structure in Seller Central, and where they conflict with the general Amazon Style Guide, the category-specific version wins. Apparel titles, for example, are usually expected to follow a fixed order: brand, department, product type, and then material or fit — deviating from that order doesn’t just look inconsistent, it can affect how the listing gets categorized.

What to do if a title is already suppressed

Check the Account Health dashboard or the notification in Seller Central for the specific rule that was flagged — Amazon usually names it. Fix only that issue rather than rewriting the whole title, since a full rewrite resets any keyword history the original title had built up. Resubmit and the listing typically reactivates within a few hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Amazon warn sellers before suppressing a title?

No. Suppression is automated and happens the moment a rule is broken, whether at initial listing creation or after an update. The first notice is usually the suppression itself, visible in Account Health.

Can a previously approved title get suppressed later?

Yes. Amazon periodically updates its style guides and re-scans existing listings against the new rules, so a title that was compliant a year ago can get flagged without the seller changing anything.

Are the title guidelines the same across all Amazon marketplaces?

No. The general structure is similar, but character limits and specific banned terms can differ between amazon.com, amazon.co.uk, and other marketplaces, so a title built for one storefront needs a check before reuse on another.

Where do I find the exact rules for my product category?

In Seller Central, under the Style Guide for your specific category — not the general Amazon title guidelines, which only cover the baseline rules that apply everywhere.
